# Inject the expert graph through interventions first

A known causal graph can enter a pixel world model in five ways: as GNN adjacency, as per-output parent masks, as attention masks, as variable-aligned latent blocks, or as a soft penalty on absent edges. For this project the soft, intervention-based route should come first, because every hard-wiring route needs the latent to be organised by named variable, and JEPA and RSSM latents are not. The notes found **no published work that wires a variable-level expert graph into a JEPA predictor or the DreamerV3 RSSM**; existing graph priors in Dreamer-style models are coarse block splits (controllable versus exogenous, reward-relevant versus not). The evidence also sets expectations: on small, dense systems such as cart-pole, a correct graph prior barely moves in-distribution one-step error, and its measurable benefit sits in invariance to non-parents, distractor robustness, out-of-distribution physics, low-data regimes and long rollouts. A wrong hard mask is the main hazard: one unreviewed 2026 report found a corrupted mask did worse than no mask at all, and a missing true edge is far more damaging than an extra one. The project's intervention pipeline (set true simulator state, re-render) is the asset that makes a soft prior exact, and its differential probes are the main liability, because a probe used in both the loss and the metric lets the model satisfy the prior cosmetically. The recommended order is: (1) an interventional consistency loss weighted by the strong / weak / absent classes, (2) a graph-masked auxiliary transition head in probe space, (3) a coarse block split for the Acrobot distractor and hidden physics, (4) fully variable-aligned latent blocks with a masked predictor, and (5) a latent Jacobian penalty through probe directions, kept as a cheap ablation only.

**Coverage and verification.** Three research threads fed this report: graph-structured predictors, latent alignment, and soft regularisers plus evaluation. A planned fourth thread on causal and factored model-based RL with known graphs (factored-MDP theory, CDL, GRADER, FOCUS, AdaRL, hidden-context methods, LLM-provided graph priors) **was not completed and was not freshly researched**. For that area this report relies on what the three threads happened to cover (CDL was read in full text there) and on two older project notes, where GRADER, AdaRL, CoDA and several others are recorded from memory. Factored-MDP theory, FOCUS and LLM-provided graph priors have no usable notes at all and are not discussed. Throughout, a claim is from a full-text or abstract-page read unless tagged "(search snippet)", "(recalled, not re-fetched)" or "(unreviewed preprint)".

## Nobody has wired a variable-level graph into a JEPA or an RSSM

The graph-as-wiring literature works on states or on pre-separated entities, never on entangled pixel latents. Interaction Networks and "Graph networks as learnable physics engines" take the graph as a given input (relations, or bodies and joints), and the latter includes Cartpole and Acrobot among its seven MuJoCo systems, but with body/joint graphs and results shown only in figures ([Battaglia et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1612.00222); [Sanchez-Gonzalez et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1806.01242)). CDL masks per-variable predictors on ground-truth factored state ([Wang et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2206.13452)). SPARTAN learns sparse attention over ground-truth object tokens and has no oracle-graph variant ([Lei et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.06890v3)). The one model family that hard-masks a transformer with a *known* DAG, the Causal Transformer and the DAG-aware Transformer, is tabular causal-effect estimation, not world modelling ([Vowels et al., preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.14485v4); [Liu et al., search snippet](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.10044)).

In the JEPA family, C-JEPA uses fully bidirectional attention over frozen object slots and states that it assumes and estimates no causal graph; its "influence neighbourhood" is predictive sufficiency, explicitly not causal parents ([Nam et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11389)). In the Dreamer family, the published factorisations are role-based. Denoised MDP splits the RSSM into controllable, uncontrollable and reward-irrelevant latents, with no identifiability proof ([Wang et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2206.15477)). IFactor defines four blocks graphically (action edge or not, path to reward or not), gives each its own transition model with inputs restricted to its graph parents, and proves block-wise identifiability ([Liu et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2306.06561)). A NeurIPS 2026 workshop poster splits the DreamerV3 deterministic state into an exogenous branch and an agent branch ([Zu et al.](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/170698)). The absence of a 28-edge, variable-level graph inside a JEPA predictor or RSSM prior is a negative result from a shallow search (two queries per thread), not proof of absence.

The structural reason for the gap is that a graph over named variables has nothing to attach to in an entangled latent. Knowing the graph does not fix this. CauCA, the strongest known-graph identifiability result, still needs **one perfect, known-target intervention per latent node** for element-wise identification and reaches only "mixing among ancestors" with imperfect interventions ([Liang et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.17225)). Control data does not supply that: force and torque act on accelerations, not on each state variable separately. The image-based methods that do deliver named, graph-aligned latents use per-variable labels (DEAR, CausalVAE) or known intervention targets (CITRIS) ([Shen et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2010.02637); [Lippe et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.03169)). This project has something those settings lack: free simulator state and an intervention-plus-re-render pipeline, which between them supply both labels and perfect interventions.

## Five injection routes, and what each demands of the latent

The 28 candidate edges form a bipartite lagged graph: 7 inputs (4 state variables, 3 action lags) to 4 state-change outputs. The notes' reading of the sources is that this maps most directly onto the CDL recipe, where each output variable has its own predictor and a binary row mask over per-input features, so **28 mask bits equal the 28 edges** ([Wang et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2206.13452)). Action lags need no special treatment: CDL treats the action as one more masked input, and an unreviewed technical report (ICWM) feeds lagged state and action history as further masked entries ([Ghosh](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.26336.md)).

| Route | Requires of the latent | Hard or soft | Closest precedent | Main weakness here |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| GNN adjacency | One node per variable | Hard | Interaction Networks, GN physics engines | Gains come from parameter sharing, which 7 heterogeneous inputs lack |
| Per-output parent masks | Per-variable input features | Hard | CDL; IFactor at block level | Needs variable-aligned inputs; a vector input gate cannot express the graph |
| Attention masks | One token per variable | Hard | Causal Transformer (tabular) | Multi-layer self-attention leaks along paths |
| Variable-aligned blocks | Supervised anchoring | Hard | DEAR composite prior; IFactor | Weakens the "learned from pixels" claim; velocities need history |
| Soft penalty / consistency loss | Nothing | Soft | CoDA pairs; RRR input-gradient penalty | Circularity if defined through the evaluation probe |

**GNN adjacency is the weakest fit.** Two mechanisms hide inside "GNN beats MLP": parameter sharing across repeated nodes, and restricting information flow to parents. Sanchez-Gonzalez et al. attribute their gain to sharing, report a negligible gap on Pendulum, and say the model was "less successful when there was not much opportunity for sharing" ([source](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1806.01242)). In C-SWM, removing pairwise message passing cost a few points of ranking accuracy at 10 steps (99.9 to 89.7 Hits@1 on 2D Shapes) while removing the factored state collapsed it to 24.1 ([Kipf et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1911.12247)). With four heterogeneous scalar variables there is nothing to share, so only parent restriction remains, and a per-output mask delivers that more simply.

**Masks must be per-output and single-hop.** A vector gate on the input is not a graph: ICWM's authors note that reducing a true adjacency to an input-vector gate "yields all ones" on one task because the sparsity lives in the input-by-output matrix ([Ghosh, unreviewed](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.26336.md)). Depth leaks too. SPARTAN defines effective parents through the product over layers of (A + I), so a stack of self-attention layers sharing one mask implements the transitive closure of that mask ([Lei et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.06890v3)). The Causal Transformer avoids this with cross-attention: queries are output slots, keys and values are always the raw embedded inputs, re-fed at each layer; it also drops layer normalisation because it rescales interventions ([Vowels et al., preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.14485v4)). Self-dependence needs an explicit decision. NRI and C-SWM carry it through a residual, which makes every output depend on its own past whether or not the graph has a self-edge ([Kipf et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1802.04687)). Because this project's targets are state *changes*, the residual can be dropped so the mask alone defines parents.

**In an RSSM, masking the prior is not enough.** The notes infer from the architecture that DreamerV3's deterministic GRU state is shared and unmasked, so masking only p(z | h) leaves every variable's information flowing through h. A graph-respecting RSSM needs per-block recurrent states with masked inputs, which is what IFactor does at block level. One secondary source reports that the *number* of DreamerV3 categoricals matters more than their size, which suggests that splitting 32 categoricals among blocks cuts into the sensitive resource ([TU Delft thesis, search snippet](https://repository.tudelft.nl/file/File_f28dfbde-630d-4770-9cdf-25fde13bf451)).

**Variable-aligned blocks are bought with labels.** Reported label budgets are small on static images: 0.01 to 0.5% of the dataset for model selection ([Locatello et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.01258)) and about 100 labelled samples for DEAR ([Shen et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2010.02637)). None of these measures block-wise anchoring of a recurrent world-model state. DEAR also supplies the most useful warning: with an independent prior, supervision has to exceed an unknown weight before the loss stops preferring an entangled encoder, which argues for putting the graph in the latent prior and not only adding probe losses. Its composite prior (structured on m dimensions, free Gaussian on the rest) is the template for "constrain part of the latent, leave the rest". Informed-Dreamer-style decoding of privileged state from the whole latent anchors information but does not allocate dimensions to variables ([Lambrechts et al., search snippet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11488v3)); decoding variable i only from block i is a combination the notes did not find in the literature. On the JEPA side, anti-collapse regularisers do not help: an isotropic-Gaussian target is rotation-invariant and cannot select a variable-aligned basis (the notes' reasoning, not a cited result). A 2026 preprint reports that JEPA latents can fail to represent key physical properties and that a training-time auxiliary head fixes this ([Zhu et al., unreviewed preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04264)).

**Soft priors need no latent structure, and the simulator makes them exact.** Published soft mechanisms penalise first-layer weight groups ([Tank et al., search snippet](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.05842v2)), a learned adjacency, or input gradients on inputs the output should ignore ([Ross et al., search snippet](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.03717)). Weight-group penalties do not transfer to a pixel encoder. The notes derive two routes that do. The first is an interventional, finite-difference penalty: for each absent edge j to i, resample true state s_j, re-render, encode, predict, and penalise movement in the readout of variable i. This is CoDA's counterfactual-pair construction turned into a loss ([Pitis et al., search snippet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.02863)), with the complementary term for present edges being a match to the simulator's response. The second is latent double backprop along probe directions, which is cheaper but assumes the probe direction is the causal direction for s_j, exactly what an entangled latent does not guarantee. The notes found **no paper applying either through a probe on a JEPA or RSSM latent**; both are open designs. A probe-free form also exists: match the predictor's response to the target encoder's embedding shift under the same simulator intervention, which is what a NeurIPS 2026 JEPA paper uses as an evaluation ([Subramanian, listing page only](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/165869)). For tuning, SPARTAN's constraint form is reusable: minimise the structure penalty subject to prediction error staying at or below the dense model's, because a fixed weight is fragile ([Lei et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.06890v3)).

## Three risks decide the design: wrong graph, dense graph, entangled latent

**A wrong hard mask is worse than none, and the two error types are asymmetric.** Direct evidence is thin. ICWM, a single-author technical report, reports error inflation from corrupting the mask of 1.16x (not significant) to 17.9x across six environment groups; the numbers were read from figure labels, and the statement that the corrupted mask underperformed the dense baseline in all six groups comes from a search-result summary of a PDF the researchers could not open ([Ghosh, unreviewed](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.26336.md)). NRI gives indirect, peer-reviewed support. On charged particles a learned graph with about 82% correct edges matches the true graph at one step (1.05e-3 versus 1.04e-3 MSE) and falls behind at 20 steps (7.06e-3 versus 5.71e-3); the fully connected limit costs six orders of magnitude on springs but 5% on charged particles ([Kipf et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1802.04687)). The notes' inference is that a missing true edge creates irreducible bias that compounds over rollouts, while an extra edge only returns the model toward the dense baseline; no source isolates the two. Soft priors are more forgiving but not immune: in causal discovery, a soft-constraint method is argued to adapt to erroneous priors ([Chen et al., abstract](https://mlanthology.org/aistats/2025/chen2025aistats-continuous)), while a later preprint reports that both hard and soft baselines "rigidly follow flawed priors" and supplies a reusable corruption protocol ([Wang et al., unreviewed preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.06790v1)). No controlled hard-versus-soft comparison under misspecification exists for world models.

Two project-specific sources of "wrongness" follow from this, both my inferences. First, hidden per-episode physics are true parents of every state change but are not among the 7 inputs, so a masked head fed only the 7 named inputs has a missing parent by construction; it needs an unmasked episode-context channel. AdaRL's domain-specific change factors are the nearest analogue in the older notes ([Huang et al., recalled, not re-fetched](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.02729)). Second, the absent-edge set is only valid at the time step where the graph was defined: with frame-skip or multi-step prediction the correct set is that of the k-step, transitively closed graph, so the expert graph should be re-derived from simulator Jacobians at the model's actual step.

**A dense graph leaves little for a prior to do in-distribution.** An AAAI-26 paper measured simulator Jacobians on eight DMC tasks and found **no globally zero state-Jacobian entry on CartpoleBalance** and no globally zero action-Jacobian entry anywhere; supervising an MLP with ground-truth Jacobians barely changed test error, and an L1 penalty on predicted Jacobians reduced accuracy ([Pandaram et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08086v1)). That result is for generalised coordinates and the full one-step map, so it does not settle this project's 7-by-4 parameterisation. CDL's authors say causal learning offers no advantage where the true graph is effectively dense, and find in-distribution accuracy similar across masked, GNN and monolithic models; the difference appears out of distribution, where dense models degrade severely (97.3% versus 35.7% on one task) ([Wang et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2206.13452)). SPARTAN's sparsity bought graph recovery and robustness, not lower prediction error (8.60 versus 8.83 on Pong, 0.52 versus 0.51 on Traffic), and its strongest result is the change in error when non-causal entities are removed: 24.5% against 1140.2% for a dense transformer ([Lei et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.06890v3)). ICWM adds that a correct mask can hurt under full observation "because gating inputs removes signal the model could have used", and helps mainly under partial observation ([Ghosh, unreviewed](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.26336.md)). The implication is to build the evaluation around the edges the graph excludes. The Acrobot distractor is the richest source of such edges, and cart-pole is where a null result on prediction error should be expected and reported as such.

**Entangled latents turn hard priors into supervision problems and soft priors into probe problems.** Slots do not rescue this: the graph nodes are generalised coordinates and rates of one articulated body, not separable image regions, and no structured-latent paper in the notes evaluates Acrobot. Velocities add a second obstacle. Identifiability theory assumes an injective map from state to observation, which a single frame violates for velocities; the notes infer that in an RSSM a per-frame stochastic z carries positions at best, so velocity blocks must live in or be computed from the recurrent path. One ICLR 2025 paper recovers the full 4-D cart-pole state from video at MCC 0.95 with a history-conditioned encoder ([Hızlı et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.03337v2)); IFactor's modified cart-pole reports blocks for cart position and pole angle and does not mention velocities ([Liu et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2306.06561)). Even with a correct graph, block-level theory leaves within-block mixing, so angle and angular velocity can stay mixed unless labels or per-variable interventions separate them.

The probe problem is circularity. If the loss says "probe_i of the prediction must not move when s_j is intervened", the model can make the probe's readout direction insensitive while still carrying and using s_j elsewhere. A same-probe evaluation would then report perfect graph agreement. No source discusses this failure for world models. The older project notes sharpen it: with sufficiently powerful non-linear alignment maps, any network can be aligned to any algorithm, demonstrated on randomly initialised models ([Sutter et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.08802)), and MLP probes have low selectivity against control tasks ([Hewitt and Liang, search snippet](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.03368)). This project's MLP differential probes sit squarely in that critique.

## Ranked design options for this project

The ranking weighs four things: whether the option works on the existing entangled latents, whether it uses the existing pipeline, how badly it fails under a wrong edge, and how much it changes the project's "learned from pixels" claim. It is my synthesis; none of these designs has been published for a pixel JEPA or DreamerV3.

| Rank | Option | Applies to | Graph enters as | Wrong-edge failure | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Interventional consistency loss | JEPA and DreamerV3 unchanged | Per-edge loss weights | Graceful (loss can pay the penalty) | Extra renders and forward passes |
| 2 | Graph-masked auxiliary head in probe space | Both, as an added head | 4 heads x 7-bit masks | Biased head, main predictor intact | Low |
| 3 | Coarse block split: dynamics / distractor / context | Both; mainly Acrobot | Block-level input restriction | Low (few, confident edges) | Moderate architecture change |
| 4 | Variable-aligned blocks with masked predictor | Both, rebuilt | Full 28-bit mask | Severe for missing edges | High; changes the claim |
| 5 | Latent Jacobian penalty via probe directions | Both | Penalty on probe-direction VJPs | Graceful but probe-bound | Lowest |

**First, the interventional consistency loss.** For each training state, pick an absent edge j to i, resample s_j in the simulator with everything else fixed, re-render both frame histories, and penalise the difference in the readout of predicted change i. For strong edges, add the complementary term that the response matches the simulator's. Weight by class: full weight for absent, a response-matching term for strong, zero or a small weight for weak, since weak edges are where the expert graph is least certain. Set the multiplier by constraint, keeping latent prediction error at or below the dense model's. Run a probe-free twin alongside it that matches the predictor's latent response to the target-encoder (JEPA) or posterior (Dreamer) shift under the same intervention; this twin teaches full interventional responses and not the graph specifically, so it serves as the "interventional data without a graph" control. The Acrobot distractor fits directly: every distractor-to-physical edge is absent and can be intervened on by re-rendering. Because velocities are history-only, an intervention on a velocity must re-render the frame history consistently, not a single frame.

**Second, a graph-masked auxiliary transition head.** Add a head that maps probe-space estimates of the 4 state variables plus the 3 action lags to the 4 state changes, built as four per-output networks with 7-bit row masks, no residual, and an unmasked episode-context input for hidden physics. Train it jointly so its gradients shape the latent. This is the CDL recipe transplanted to probe space and is the cheapest test of a *hard* prior. Its limits should be stated up front: it constrains the head, not the world model's own predictor, and a head can be right in-distribution while the latent still routes non-parent information elsewhere. Pair it with option 1's held-out intervention tests.

**Third, a coarse block split.** Give the distractor its own latent block whose transition receives neither actions nor the physical block, and give hidden physics a slowly varying context block with no action input. This is the IFactor mechanism with a graph-defined partition and is the only hard prior with direct Dreamer precedent, including a cart-pole variant with an uncontrollable distractor cart-pole. It uses only the most confident edges of the expert graph, so its wrong-graph exposure is small. One caution from the older notes applies to any shortcut for discovering the split: multi-step inverse models fail under periodic dynamics ([Levine et al., search snippet](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.11940v2)), and both systems are pendulum-like, so the partition should be imposed from the graph, not learned by inverse dynamics.

**Fourth, variable-aligned blocks with a masked predictor.** Allocate one block per state variable plus distractor, context and a free residual block; anchor each by decoding variable i only from block i with a swept label fraction; then mask the predictor. For JEPA use Causal-Transformer-style cross-attention from input tokens to output tokens with action lags as separate tokens (C-JEPA found separate action tokens beat concatenation) and no layer norm on the masked path. For DreamerV3 use per-block recurrent states with masked inputs. This has the highest ceiling and is the only route where the model's own dynamics obey the graph by construction, but it converts the study from "does a pixel world model learn the graph" to "can one be built to", so it belongs as an upper-reference arm, with the label fraction reported.

**Fifth, the latent Jacobian penalty through probe directions**, as an ablation that isolates how much of option 1's effect needs real interventions. A graph-shaped GNN predictor with shared node functions is not recommended at all.

## An evaluation protocol that separates graph benefit from probe artefact

**Pre-checks.** Re-derive the expert graph from simulator Jacobians at the model's actual frame-skip and parameterisation, and count the absent edges per environment; if cart-pole has very few, declare in advance that its prediction-error comparison is expected to be null. Train a state-input masked model as an oracle upper bound.

**Conditions.** Every arm uses identical architecture, data and seeds. The dense baseline (penalty weight zero) and the oracle bracket the comparison. The graph arms are the expert graph, the project's recovered graph, a shuffled graph with matched edge count (which separates "the right graph" from "any sparsity"), and a corruption dose-response run **separately for deleted true edges and added false edges**, a separation no source in the notes performs. A matched-strength generic regulariser, such as an unstructured Jacobian penalty ([Hoffman et al., mechanism recalled, not re-fetched](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.02729)), separates graph-specific benefit from smoothness. The probe-free interventional-matching arm separates the graph from interventional data. Finally, sweep dataset size, since the one world-model study that looked found the graph helps under limited coverage and partial observation and not under full matched data.

**Metrics, ordered from probe-free to probe-dependent.** First, latent prediction error at one step and over rollouts, since differences from a wrong graph show at longer horizons. Second, the stage-by-stage intervention trace: the same simulator intervention followed through state, rendered frames, target embeddings and predictor outputs, with an isotropic perturbation matched in size as a control for a dead or globally shrunk predictor. Third, SPARTAN's robustness metric applied to the Acrobot distractor: percentage change in prediction error when the distractor is altered or removed. Fourth, held-out physics, using the existing per-episode variation with a held-out range; a causal-RL survey's cart-pole variants (pole length doubled, pole mass tripled) give a published reference, though for state-based PPO ([da Costa Cunha et al., unreviewed preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.18135)). Fifth, counterfactual-query accuracy from the existing pipeline. Sixth, recovered-graph agreement against the expert graph, reported threshold-free (AUROC, AUPRC) or with the project's null-calibrated family-wise threshold, because SPARTAN documents that post hoc thresholds become oracle choices.

**Circularity controls for anything probe-based.** Freeze the trained model and fit fresh probes with a different seed, data split and probe class from those used in the loss. Report probe selectivity against a control task. Run the full pipeline on an untrained model as a floor. Evaluate on interventions held out from training: unseen magnitudes, unseen state regions, and a held-out subset of absent edges, which tests whether invariance generalises or was memorised per edge. Where interchange-style tests are added, restrict alignment maps to linear ones and score both "cause" and "isolate", so that an intervention on angular velocity is credited only if it leaves the angle readout unchanged ([Huang et al., recalled, not re-fetched](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17700)).

**Statistics.** Pair seeds across conditions and use Wilcoxon signed-rank tests on paired runs, as the mask-corruption study does, or interquartile means with stratified-bootstrap intervals ([Agarwal et al., recalled, not re-fetched](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.13264)). Three to five seeds with confidence intervals is the weak end of current practice.

## Conclusion

The literature inverts the obvious plan. Hard-wiring the graph looks principled but presupposes the variable-aligned latent that is the hard part, and on a dense four-variable system it buys little that prediction error can show. What this project uniquely holds is a simulator that answers any interventional question exactly, which turns the expert graph from an architectural constraint into a set of testable invariances that can be taught, withheld and corrupted at will. That same asset makes the cleanest contribution available: a hard-versus-soft-versus-dense comparison under controlled missing-edge and extra-edge corruption in a pixel world model, which the notes found nowhere.

Two things would change the ranking. If fresh probes on the unmodified models already show well-separated variable subspaces, option 4 becomes cheap and moves up. If the Jacobian pre-check shows cart-pole has almost no absent edges at the model's time step, the cart-pole arm should be reframed as a negative control and the Acrobot distractor and hidden-physics splits should carry the claims. The incomplete fourth thread matters most for hidden physics: how context-conditioned and factored-MDP methods treat per-episode parameters as graph nodes is the largest unresearched question behind options 2 and 3.
