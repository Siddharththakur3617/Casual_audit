# Latent alignment: structuring a pixel world model's latent space so blocks correspond to nodes of a GIVEN causal graph (JEPA and DreamerV3/RSSM focus)

Notes compiled 2026-11-16. Verification convention: every bullet under "Cited Findings" was read in this session from the linked page (arXiv abstract page, arXiv/ar5iv HTML full text, or a search-result summary, which is flagged as "search snippet"). Anything I know only from background knowledge and did not re-open is placed under "Gaps" and labelled UNVERIFIED. Full-text reads were truncated at about 100k characters per paper, so appendices were generally not read.

## 1. Causal representation learning with a KNOWN graph or known structure: assumptions and what is identifiable

### Takeaway
Knowing the graph does not by itself anchor latents to variables: the strongest known-graph result (CauCA) still needs one perfect intervention per node with known targets to get element-wise identification, and only reaches "mixing among ancestors" with imperfect interventions. The methods that actually deliver named, graph-aligned latents on images (CausalVAE, DEAR) do it with per-variable labels, and the temporal methods (CITRIS, BISCUIT, TDRL/LEAP, IFactor) replace labels with intervention targets, actions, or non-stationarity and identify variables only up to permutation and element-wise transforms, or only block-wise.

### Cited Findings

**Causal Component Analysis (CauCA)** — Liang Wendong, Kekić, von Kügelgen, Buchholz, Besserve, Gresele, Schölkopf; NeurIPS 2023.
- CauCA "presupposes knowledge of the causal graph, focusing solely on learning the unmixing function and the causal mechanisms"; nonlinear ICA is the special case of an empty graph. Venue per arXiv comments: NeurIPS 2023 camera-ready. — [arXiv 2305.17225](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17225)
- Setting: graph G known, intervention targets observed, mixing function a C1 diffeomorphism (deterministic, no observation noise), latent distributions Markov to G with full support. — [ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.17225)
- Theorem 4.2(i): one stochastic single-node intervention per node (perfect or imperfect) satisfying "interventional discrepancy" identifies latents only up to mixing within the closure of each node's ancestors. Theorem 4.2(ii): one perfect stochastic intervention per node identifies up to element-wise scaling (reparametrisation), described as "the best one we can hope to achieve in our problem setting without further assumptions". — [ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.17225)
- Proposition 4.3 (impossibility): d-1 perfect single-node interventions are not enough when the remaining node has a parent, so d interventions are necessary. — [ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.17225)
- Fat-hand (multi-node) interventions give only block-identifiability (Theorem 4.5). With fully unknown targets the paper states there are "fundamental and strong limits to identifiability". — [ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.17225)
- Experiments are synthetic only (random DAGs, mostly linear-Gaussian latent SCMs, MLP mixing, normalizing-flow likelihood estimation); no images or control tasks. The authors note more scalable estimators (e.g. variational) may be needed. — [ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.17225)

**DEAR** — Shen, Liu, Dong, Lian, Chen, Zhang; JMLR 23(241), 2022 (arXiv 2020).
- Uses an SCM as the prior of a bidirectional generative model trained with a GAN algorithm, with "supervised information on the ground-truth factors and their underlying causal structure". — [arXiv 2010.02637](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.02637)
- Structural knowledge required: a super-graph of the true causal graph, ranging from the true graph (best case) to only a causal ordering (worst case); DEAR learns edge weights within it, and the super-graph guarantees acyclicity with no extra constraint. — [ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2010.02637)
- Prior form: z = f((I - A^T)^-1 h(eps)), eps ~ N(0, I), A the weighted DAG adjacency; a composite prior puts the SCM on the first m dimensions (factors of interest) and a standard Gaussian on the remaining k - m dimensions. — [ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2010.02637)
- Proposition 4: with a factorised (independent) prior, the combined generative plus supervised loss can prefer an entangled encoder unless the supervision weight is large enough; the abstract states such methods "fail to disentangle causally related factors even under supervision". — [arXiv 2010.02637](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.02637); [ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2010.02637)
- Proposition 5 (identifiability): under infinite capacity and the true factor distribution lying in the SCM family, the minimiser of the DEAR objective is disentangled with respect to the true factors; this is a population-level statement. — [ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2010.02637)
- Datasets: synthetic Pendulum images (four causally related continuous factors) and CelebA. — [ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2010.02637)

**CausalVAE** — Yang, Liu, Chen, Shen, Hao, Wang (arXiv 2004.08697; the arXiv page lists no venue).
- VAE with a Causal Layer mapping independent exogenous factors to causally related endogenous ones; the graph is learned, not given (the DAG "is identified with good accuracy"); the learned model "recovers the true one up to a certain degree by providing supervision signals" such as feature labels; evaluated on synthetic data and CelebA; supports do-operation counterfactual generation. — [arXiv 2004.08697](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.08697)
- The DEAR paper characterises CausalVAE as building on iVAE with a conditional prior given the ground-truth factors, and hence limited to the fully supervised setting. — [ar5iv DEAR full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2010.02637)

**CITRIS** — Lippe, Magliacane, Löwe, Asano, Cohen, Gavves; ICML 2022.
- Learns causal representations from temporal image sequences where factors may be intervened on, exploiting observed intervention targets; handles multidimensional causal variables (e.g. 3D rotation); proves identifiability in a more general case where only some components of a factor are affected by interventions; a normalizing flow lets it disentangle the latents of an already-pretrained autoencoder; experiments on 3D rendered image sequences. — [arXiv 2202.03169](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.03169)

**BISCUIT** — Lippe, Magliacane, Löwe, Asano, Cohen, Gavves; UAI 2023.
- Supervision is an agent's interactions (actions) whose effect on each causal variable is an unknown binary variable; each variable is assumed to have two mechanisms (observational and interventional); causal variables are identifiable under these assumptions "for many common setups" (additive Gaussian noise given as an example); evaluated on three robotic-inspired datasets. — [arXiv 2306.09643](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09643)

**TDRL and LEAP** — Yao, Chen, Zhang; NeurIPS 2022 (TDRL).
- TDRL recovers nonparametric time-delayed latent causal variables and their relations from sequences, under stationary environments and distribution shifts; LEAP, which it extends, established identifiability only in limited non-stationary cases where the noise distribution varies across all segments. (Search snippet of the NeurIPS/arXiv record.) — [arXiv 2210.13647](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2210.13647); [NeurIPS 2022 proceedings](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/hash/a938292feb86b94ebe3e6200ff7786ef-Abstract-Conference.html)

**Identifying latent state transitions (cart-pole from video)** — Hızlı, Yıldız, Bethge, John, Marttinen; ICLR 2025.
- Cart-pole with observations that "consist of video sequences of the system"; true states used only to compute MCC. Latent states identified up to permutation and element-wise invertible transformation (Theorem 1, from Yao et al. 2021); the paper's contribution is identifying process noise and the transition function up to the same ambiguity (Theorem 2). Assumptions include an injective generator, bijective augmented dynamics, one noise variable per latent dimension, and sufficient variability with respect to an auxiliary variable u (regime index or previous noise). Cart-pole MCC 0.95 on latent states versus 0.83 (LEAP-Lin), 0.80 (LEAP-NP), 0.62 (PCL), 0.60 (KalmanVAE), 0.54 (SlowVAE). Authors' stated limitations: results depend on the assumptions, simulator only, no downstream model-based policy learning shown. — [arXiv 2406.03337 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.03337v2); [ICLR 2025 proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/b192f601432dff4e6777d2d06d52cfa5-Abstract-Conference.html)

**Variational Causal Dynamics (VCD)** — Lei, Schölkopf, Posner; arXiv 2022 (a related NeurIPS 2022 workshop poster exists).
- Latent state-space model with a structural transition model: each latent dimension is treated as a causal variable predicted only from its parents in a learned graph, with a sparsity penalty; evaluated on simulated environments with state and image observations; uses data from several intervened environments. Here the graph is learned, not given. (Search snippet.) — [arXiv 2206.11131](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.11131); [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2206.11131)

### Inferences
- For a cart-pole or Acrobot world model, the given graph alone leaves the encoder unidentified: CauCA's theory says graph plus observational (non-intervened) data is insufficient, and its sufficient condition (one perfect, known-target intervention per latent node) is not what a control dataset provides, since torque/force actions intervene on accelerations, not on each state variable separately.
- All temporal identifiability results above leave a permutation plus element-wise invertible ambiguity. A graph over named variables needs that permutation fixed, which in every image-based method found here is done by labels (CausalVAE, DEAR) or by known intervention targets per variable (CITRIS, CauCA).
- DEAR's composite prior (SCM on m dimensions, free Gaussian on the rest) and its super-graph mask are the closest existing template for "impose a known graph on part of the latent, leave the rest unconstrained", but it is a static image model with no dynamics or actions.
- DEAR's Proposition 4 is directly relevant to world models with independent latent priors: supervised anchoring fights the prior when true variables are causally correlated, which argues for putting the graph into the latent prior and not only adding probe losses.

### Gaps
- UNVERIFIED (background knowledge, not re-opened): CausalVAE was published at CVPR 2021; the arXiv page does not list the venue.
- UNVERIFIED: iCITRIS (Lippe et al., ICLR 2023, arXiv 2206.06169) extends CITRIS to instantaneous effects; not opened. CITRIS's own treatment of instantaneous effects and "minimal causal variables" was not confirmed from the abstract page.
- UNVERIFIED: Schölkopf et al., "Toward Causal Representation Learning" (Proceedings of the IEEE, 2021, arXiv 2102.11107) was not opened; no claims are taken from it.
- SCM-VAE, ICM-VAE (a search result titled "Learning Causally Disentangled Representations via the Principle of Independent Causal Mechanisms", arXiv 2306.01213, appeared but was not read), and GraphVAE-style priors were not researched; no findings.
- CauCA's Appendix D/E (parents-closure result, matched targets) were only seen via the main-text summary.
- No paper was found that proves identifiability for "known temporal graph over latent state variables plus actions, unknown mixing from pixels" as in a control setting.

## 2. How much label supervision is needed to anchor latent blocks to named variables

### Takeaway
Reported label budgets for anchoring are small: 0.01-0.5% of the dataset for model selection (Locatello et al.), about 100 labelled samples for DEAR, and a handful of annotated frames for object-centric RL (OC-STORM); in RL world models, privileged simulator state is typically used as a training-only decoding target (Informed Dreamer and successors), which is an anchoring mechanism but not one designed to produce variable-aligned blocks.

### Cited Findings
- Locatello, Tschannen, Bauer, Rätsch, Schölkopf, Bachem, "Disentangling Factors of Variation Using Few Labels", ICLR 2020: "a small number of labeled examples (0.01--0.5% of the data set)" suffices for model selection among unsupervised disentanglement methods; labels may be "imprecise and incomplete"; over 52,000 models trained; they also study adding supervision during training. — [arXiv 1905.01258](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.01258)
- DEAR: labels are needed only for a subset of samples; performance is reported competitive with as few as about 100 labelled samples; a DEAR-10% variant uses 10% of labels with some loss of accuracy. — [ar5iv DEAR full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2010.02637)
- DEAR Proposition 4: with an independent prior, supervision must be weighted above an unknown threshold to overcome the prior, and very large weights make the generative model hard to train. — [ar5iv DEAR full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2010.02637)
- CausalVAE's identifiability relies on supervision signals such as feature labels. — [arXiv 2004.08697](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.08697)
- Informed POMDP / Informed Dreamer (Lambrechts, Bolland, Ernst; Reinforcement Learning Journal / RLC 2024): formalises information available only at training time; adapts Dreamer with an information decoder that predicts the privileged information from the latent state; the abstract reports convergence speed "sometimes greatly improved". (Search snippet.) — [arXiv 2306.11488](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11488v3); [RLJ 2024](https://rlj.cs.umass.edu/2024/papers/Paper105.html)
- Reinformed Dreamer (preprint, 2026): argues the Informed Dreamer objective for the privileged latent is flawed and introduces a privileged variational encoder (two encoders in one world model); reports matching or better convergence and final performance. (Search snippet; preprint.) — [arXiv 2607.26040](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.26040)
- SkyDreamer (preprint, 2025): decodes privileged observations such as ground-truth state in its world model, following Informed Dreamer, for vision-based drone racing. (Search snippet.) — [arXiv 2510.14783](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.14783)
- TWIST (2023): a teacher world model trained on simulator state supervises an image-based student world model for sim-to-real. (Search snippet.) — [arXiv 2311.03622](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.03622.pdf)
- OC-STORM (Zhang, Jelley, McInroe, Storkey; ICLR 2026, originally "Objects matter", arXiv 2501.16443): annotates key objects with a few segmentation masks, extracts object features with a frozen pretrained segmentation model (Cutie, SAM2), feeds them with raw observations into a STORM world model; beats STORM on 18 of 26 Atari 100k games. (Search snippet.) — [arXiv 2501.16443](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.16443v1); [ICLR 2026 entry](https://mlanthology.org/iclr/2026/zhang2026iclr-objectcentric/)
- "Spectral-Target Physical Latent Structuring for JEPA-Style World Models" (Zhu et al., preprint, Sept 2026): a training-time auxiliary head (Fourier head) on LeWorldModel latents raises correlation between latents and physical properties and planning success, with no inference cost, and helps most in low-data regimes. — [arXiv 2609.04264](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04264)

### Inferences
- The evidence supports auxiliary-regression anchoring with a small labelled subset as practical (hundreds of labelled frames is within reported ranges), but none of the cited results measures the label budget needed for block-wise anchoring of a recurrent world-model state on a control task; the numbers come from static image datasets (dSprites-style, Pendulum, CelebA).
- Informed-Dreamer-style decoding of the full simulator state from the whole latent does not allocate particular latent dimensions to particular variables; to get graph-aligned blocks one would have to decode variable i only from block i (a concept-bottleneck-style constraint). This combination was not found in the literature searched.
- Because the simulator state is free in cart-pole/Acrobot, the label budget is a design variable, not a cost; the relevant question becomes how little supervision keeps the claim "learned from pixels" meaningful.

### Gaps
- UNVERIFIED: Locatello et al., "Challenging Common Assumptions in the Unsupervised Learning of Disentangled Representations" (ICML 2019, arXiv 1811.12359), the impossibility result for unsupervised disentanglement without inductive bias, was not opened.
- UNVERIFIED: concept-bottleneck models (Koh et al., ICML 2020, arXiv 2007.04612) were not opened; no findings on concept leakage or label requirements.
- "Scaffolder" (privileged sensing for Dreamer-style MBRL) did not appear in search; not covered.
- No source found that reports label-efficiency curves for anchoring RSSM or JEPA latents to named physical state variables.

## 3. Factored / structured latents in Dreamer-family models

### Takeaway
Dreamer-family factorisations that exist are coarse, role-based partitions (controllable vs. not, reward-relevant vs. not) implemented as separate latent blocks with restricted transition inputs (TIA, Denoised MDP, IFactor, Iso-Dream), or object slots (SOLD, OC-STORM, STICA). I found no published work that assigns DreamerV3's categorical groups to named physical variables or places a given variable-level graph mask on the RSSM prior or GRU input.

### Cited Findings
- Denoised MDP (Wang, Du, Torralba, Isola, Zhang, Tian; arXiv 2206.15477): built on Dreamer's framework with the RSSM as base; three latents: x (controllable, reward-relevant; transition depends only on past x and action), y (uncontrollable; evolves independently of actions and x), z (reward-irrelevant; may depend on x, y, z and action but affects only itself); observation emitted from (x, y, z); loss is reconstruction plus per-block KL terms plus a mutual-information regulariser I(x; s | a) keeping x minimal. Experiments use a variant without z. — [ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2206.15477); [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.15477)
- Denoised MDP gives no identifiability proof: x could in principle hold all of s, so the split is not unique and relies on the regulariser. — [ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2206.15477)
- IFactor (Liu, Huang, Zhu, Tian, Gong, Yu, Zhang; arXiv 2306.06561): four blocks defined graphically: s^{ar} (edge from a_{t-1}, path to r_t), s^{a-bar r} (no action edge, path to reward), s^{a r-bar} (action edge, no path to reward), s^{a-bar r-bar} (neither). Built on Dreamer; each block has its own transition and representation model; transitions are masked: s^{ar} depends on previous s^r and a; s^{a-bar r} only on previous s^r; s^{a r-bar} on the previous full state and a; s^{a-bar r-bar} on the previous full state. Loss adds per-block KL weights and two MINE-style mutual-information objectives (reward predicted from s^r not s^{r-bar}; action predicted from s^a not s^{a-bar}). Policy uses only s^r. — [ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2306.06561); [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06561)
- TIA (Fu, Yang, Agrawal, Jaakkola; ICML 2021): uses reward to factor the MDP into a task-relevant and a task-irrelevant world model that jointly reconstruct the observation; the distractor model is pushed away from reward-correlated features. (Search snippet.) — [ICML 2021 slides](https://ICML.cc/media/icml-2021/Slides/9551.pdf); description in [Denoised MDP full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2206.15477)
- Iso-Dream (Pan, Zhu, Wang, Yang; NeurIPS 2022): extends Dream-to-Control; inverse dynamics separates controllable and noncontrollable dynamics into isolated state-transition branches; noncontrollable states are rolled forward and used in value estimation. (Search snippet.) — [arXiv 2205.13817](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.13817); [NeurIPS 2022 poster](https://nips.cc/virtual/2022/poster/54234)
- RePo (Zhu, Simchowitz, Gadipudi, Gupta; NeurIPS 2023): a modification of the RSSM that drops reconstruction, makes the latent maximally predictive of dynamics and reward while constraining information flow from observation to latent. This removes distractors without factorising the latent. (Search snippet.) — [arXiv 2309.00082](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.00082); [NeurIPS 2023 poster](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/71822)
- SOLD (Mosbach, Ewertz, Villar-Corrales, Behnke; arXiv 2410.08822, preprint per the arXiv page): model-based RL with slot-attention object-centric latent dynamics learned unsupervised from pixels; reports outperforming DreamerV3 and TD-MPC2 on robotic relational manipulation tasks. — [arXiv 2410.08822](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08822)
- STICA (arXiv 2511.14262, preprint): object-centric slot latents with a causal graph and causality scores guiding policy and value networks; the search result did not show a sparse mask inside the RSSM itself. (Search snippet.) — [arXiv 2511.14262](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.14262v1)
- Variational Sparse Gating (built on the Dreamer RSSM): updates latent feature dimensions sparsely through stochastic binary gates; sparsity is in time/updates, not a variable-to-variable graph. (Search snippet; venue not confirmed.) — [search result set](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/file/0a97df4ce5b403ea87645010e9005130-Paper-Conference.pdf)
- DreamerV3 categoricals: the RSSM state is a deterministic recurrent state plus a stochastic categorical state sampled with straight-through gradients and unimix (TorchRL docs); a TU Delft thesis reports that the number of categorical distributions matters more for performance than their size. Neither is an interpretability result. (Search snippet; secondary sources.) — [TorchRL DreamerV3 docs](https://docs.pytorch.org/rl/main/reference/dreamer_v3.html); [TU Delft thesis](https://repository.tudelft.nl/file/File_f28dfbde-630d-4770-9cdf-25fde13bf451)
- A Stanford CS224R student project ("Set Dreamer") tried a set-based abstraction over DreamerV3 latent factors and reported it underperformed default DreamerV3. (Search snippet; student project, weak source.) — [CS224R project PDF](https://cs224r.stanford.edu/projects/pdfs/Set_Dreamer_CS224R_Final_Project.pdf)
- A targeted search for a factored RSSM with a sparse causal mask returned no paper matching that design; nearest hits were STICA, VSG and Hybrid-RSSM (masking for representation learning, arXiv 2405.06263). — [arXiv 2405.06263](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.06263v1)

### Inferences
- IFactor is the clearest precedent that a hand-specified graph can be compiled into a Dreamer world model: each block gets its own prior network whose inputs are restricted to the block's graph parents. Extending the same mechanism from four role blocks to per-variable blocks (x, x-dot, theta, theta-dot) with the expert graph as the input mask is architecturally straightforward, but it is an extrapolation; nobody was found to have done it or shown it trains well.
- In DreamerV3 the deterministic GRU state h is shared and unmasked, so masking only p(z_t | h_t) would not enforce a graph: information from all variables flows through h. A graph-respecting design needs either per-block recurrent states (as in IFactor's separate transition models) or masked GRU inputs; this follows from the architecture description, not from a cited experiment.
- Assigning groups of the 32 categoricals to variables is unprecedented in the sources found; the only relevant empirical hint is that the count of categoricals matters more than their size, which suggests splitting 32 categoricals among 4-6 blocks cuts into the more sensitive resource.

### Gaps
- Venues: Denoised MDP (believed ICML 2022) and IFactor (believed NeurIPS 2023) are UNVERIFIED; the arXiv pages list no venue (the Denoised MDP text only carries "ICML" as a keyword). SOLD may have a later conference version; not checked.
- FOCUS (object-centric world model for manipulation) and "Dreamer with slots" variants other than SOLD/OC-STORM/STICA were not researched.
- No peer-reviewed probing study of whether DreamerV3 categoricals spontaneously align with state variables was found.
- The absence of a graph-masked RSSM is a negative search result from two queries, not proof of absence.

## 4. Structured latents in JEPA-family models

### Takeaway
JEPA structuring to date is object-level (C-JEPA: frozen slot encoder, object-masked prediction, actions as separate tokens) or regulariser-level (VICReg/SIGReg anti-collapse terms); C-JEPA explicitly uses no causal graph and claims only predictive sufficiency, and no JEPA work found aligns latent blocks with named state variables of a given graph.

### Cited Findings
- Causal-JEPA / C-JEPA (Nam, Le Lidec, Maes, LeCun, Balestriero; arXiv 2602.11389; the arXiv page states acceptance at ICML 2026): extends masked joint-embedding prediction from patches to object-level representations; about 20% absolute gain in counterfactual reasoning over the same architecture without object-level masking; comparable control performance to patch-based world models with about 1% of latent input features. — [arXiv 2602.11389](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11389)
- C-JEPA details: a frozen object-centric encoder maps each frame to N slots (VideoSAUR over frozen DINOv2 features; SAVi for baseline comparability); 7 slots for CLEVRER, 4 for Push-T. Selected object slots are masked across the history, future slots always masked; the earliest step is kept as an "identity anchor" because slots are permutation-equivariant. Actions and proprioception are separate entity tokens conditioning the predictor, reported to outperform concatenation as in DINO-WM. — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11389v2)
- C-JEPA theory: defines an "influence neighborhood" (minimal subset of other variables giving predictive equivalence); Theorem 1 says the optimal predictor must use it; the authors stress this is predictive sufficiency, not identified causal parents; the model works "without requiring an explicit causal graph". — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11389v2)
- C-JEPA numbers: CLEVRER (VideoSAUR) average 82.79% unmasked versus 89.40% with 4 masked objects, counterfactual per-question 47.68% versus 68.81%; Push-T planning success: DINO-WM 91.33% (196x384 tokens), OC-DINO-WM 60.67%, OC-JEPA 76.00%, C-JEPA 88.67% (6x128 tokens), 1.02% of tokens and about 8x faster planning. — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11389v2)
- C-JEPA stated limitations: performance is capped by the frozen object-centric encoder; influence-neighborhood claims not validated on data with known temporal causal graphs; joint encoder training without collapse is future work. — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11389v2)
- IA-JEPA ("Entity-Centric World Models: Interaction-Aware Masking for Causal Video Prediction", single author S. K. Paidi; arXiv 2605.15466, preprint, no venue): self-supervised motion/interaction-centric masking; CLEVRER causal reasoning accuracy 14.26% versus 3.22% for patch-masked baselines; claims about 10% latent entropy gain and that latents linearise physical energy with R2 = 0.43. — [arXiv 2605.15466](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15466)
- PLDM (Sobal, Zhang, Cho, Balestriero, Rudner, LeCun; NeurIPS 2025): JEPA latent dynamics from reward-free offline data; collapse prevented by a VICReg-style objective plus inverse dynamics modelling; planning by MPPI; evaluated on navigation tasks across 23 datasets. (Search snippet.) — [arXiv 2502.14819](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.14819); [NeurIPS 2025 poster](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/poster/116649)
- DINO-WM freezes a DINOv2 encoder and models dynamics over its patch features; "What Drives Success in Physical Planning with Joint-Embedding Predictive World Models?" (arXiv 2512.24497, preprint) ablates predictor design, context length, proprioception and planner and proposes a variant beating DINO-WM and V-JEPA-2-AC. (Search snippet.) — [arXiv 2512.24497](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.24497v3)
- LeWorldModel (arXiv 2603.19312, preprint): end-to-end JEPA world model from pixels with SIGReg, a regulariser pushing embeddings toward an isotropic Gaussian; 15M parameters; reported about 48x faster planning than DINO-WM with about 200x fewer tokens. (Search snippet.) — [arXiv 2603.19312](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19312)
- "Physical representation laziness" (Zhu et al., arXiv 2609.04264, preprint): LeWM latents do not collapse but can fail to represent key physical properties, causing planning failures in highly dynamic environments; training-time auxiliary supervision fixes this and raises latent-to-physical-property correlation. — [arXiv 2609.04264](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04264)

### Inferences
- JEPA anti-collapse regularisers (VICReg covariance, SIGReg isotropy) decorrelate or whiten dimensions but nothing in the sources claims they make dimensions correspond to physical variables; an isotropic Gaussian target is rotation-invariant, so it cannot by itself select a variable-aligned basis (reasoning from the stated objective, not a cited result).
- C-JEPA's design choices transfer to a variable-aligned JEPA: per-entity tokens, actions as separate tokens, entity-level masking, and an attention pattern that could be hard-masked by a given graph. But its entities come from a frozen object segmenter, which has no analogue for "pole angular velocity"; the token-to-variable binding would have to come from supervision.
- The laziness result (arXiv 2609.04264) is the closest JEPA evidence that auxiliary physical-state supervision is needed and sufficient to make JEPA latents carry physical variables.

### Gaps
- UNVERIFIED and not opened: V-JEPA 2 / V-JEPA 2-AC primary paper (Meta, 2025), LeJEPA primary paper (Balestriero and LeCun, 2025), DINO-WM primary paper (Zhou et al.). Only second-hand descriptions are cited above.
- No work was found on block-wise prediction or disentangled, variable-aligned JEPA latents for articulated control; the search for "disentanglement JEPA latent state variables probing" returned nothing direct.

## 5. Velocity variables: variables identifiable only from history

### Takeaway
None of the structured-latent methods found treats velocities as a special class; they either rely on a recurrent/history-conditioned encoder so that all state variables (including velocities) can be inferred, or are evaluated on per-frame-visible factors. The cart-pole video identifiability paper recovers the 4-D state [x, x-dot, theta, theta-dot] with a history-conditioned encoder but does not explain how velocities are obtained.

### Cited Findings
- Hızlı et al. (ICLR 2025): the hidden cart-pole state is cart position and velocity plus pole angle and angular velocity, observed as video; the encoder q(s_t | z_{t-1}, x_{1:t}) conditions on all frames up to t; the general encoder description uses 4 frames for the initial condition but the cart-pole setup uses only the first frame, an inconsistency the paper does not resolve; MCC 0.95 on latent states. — [arXiv 2406.03337 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.03337v2)
- A source surfaced in search states that pendulum state variables "cannot be measured from a single video frame alone", i.e. one frame is insufficient for the full state. (Search snippet; the specific source among the results was not opened.) — [search hit: arXiv 2410.18539](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2410.18539)
- "Physics from Video: Identifiability of Time-Invariant Second-Order ODEs under Minimal Trajectory Conditions" (reported as ICML 2026): underdamped systems identifiable from a single video, other damping regimes need three distinct trajectories; notes an encoder-only setup risks latent collapse. (Search snippet from a secondary summary site; low confidence.) — [papernotes summary](https://en.papernotes.org/ICML2026/interpretability/physics_from_video_identifiability_of_time-invariant_second-order_odes_under_min/)
- IFactor assumes only time-delayed effects, with no instantaneous causal relations among latents; instantaneous effects and low-resolution time series are left to future work. — [ar5iv IFactor full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2306.06561)
- IFactor's modified cart-pole result reports blocks corresponding to cart position and pole angle (plus a light and a distractor cart-pole); velocities are not mentioned in the block interpretation. — [ar5iv IFactor full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2306.06561)
- Denoised MDP argues partial observability is handled in principle (recurrent model) but gives no formal guarantee. — [ar5iv Denoised MDP full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2206.15477)
- C-JEPA's assumptions include finite-history sufficiency and explicitly do not assume first-order Markov dynamics; object slots are per-frame and motion is recovered by the predictor over a history window. — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11389v2)
- TRACE (Fan, Zhang, Cheng; ICML 2026 per arXiv page): a search snippet reported a pixel CartPole experiment (128x128 grayscale) with MCC 0.970 "on the visually observable components"; the abstract page itself does not mention CartPole, so this is unconfirmed. — [arXiv 2601.21135](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.21135)

### Inferences
- The identifiability theories used here assume an injective generator from latent state to observation. With single-frame observations the map from [x, x-dot, theta, theta-dot] to a frame is not injective in the velocities, so the assumption holds only if the "observation" is a frame stack or the encoder is recurrent. This is an inference from the stated assumptions; the papers do not discuss it.
- In an RSSM, per-step stochastic z_t inferred from one frame can at best carry positions; velocities must live in the deterministic history state h_t (or z must be inferred from h_t plus the frame). A variable-aligned design therefore needs velocity blocks located in, or computed from, the recurrent path, with the graph edge position_{t-1}, position_t -> velocity_t made explicit. No cited paper does this.
- The TRACE snippet's phrase "visually observable components" suggests that at least one recent CRL evaluation scores only positions/angles; if confirmed, it is direct evidence that velocities are treated as outside the identifiable set from frames.

### Gaps
- No source found that explicitly distinguishes "instantaneously observable" from "history-only" variables in a structured-latent world model, or evaluates per-variable recovery of velocities versus positions in Dreamer/JEPA.
- How CITRIS/BISCUIT handle velocity-like variables was not checked (their benchmarks are believed to involve per-frame-visible factors; UNVERIFIED).
- The TRACE CartPole numbers need confirmation from the paper body.

## 6. Distractors and hidden physics: controllable / reward-relevant / exogenous blocks, and how a known graph specifies the partition

### Takeaway
The partition is defined graphically in both Denoised MDP and IFactor (existence of an action edge and of a path to reward), so a known graph specifies it directly; IFactor proves block-wise identifiability under invertibility and Jacobian conditions, Denoised MDP has no guarantee, and the AC-State line identifies the controllable latent via multi-step inverse models under deterministic Ex-BMDP assumptions that have known failure cases.

### Cited Findings
- Denoised MDP: information is split by controllability and reward relevance; "useful information" is "both controllable and reward-relevant"; benchmarks are DMC (Cheetah Run, Walker Walk, Reacher Easy) with video background, noisy sensor and camera jitter variants, and a modified RoboDesk (TV, flickering lights, camera jitter, noisy sensors), five runs, plus a joint-position regression task. — [arXiv 2206.15477](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.15477); [ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2206.15477)
- Denoised MDP on TIA: TIA assumes independent signal and noise latent MDPs composed with a pixel mask, so it fails on reward-relevant noise (sensor noise) and on camera jitter, which masking cannot represent. — [ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2206.15477)
- IFactor block-wise identifiability (Theorem 1) assumes a smooth invertible generating map with smooth inverse, a Jacobian non-degeneracy condition, and full-support latents; the result is block-wise, not component-wise. — [ar5iv IFactor full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2306.06561)
- IFactor results: synthetic R2 above 0.9 in both directions for all four blocks while Denoised MDP, BetaVAE, FactorVAE, SlowVAE, PCL and TDRL show distorted recovery; modified CartPole recovers cart position as s^{ar}, pole angle as s^{a-bar r}, a green light as s^{a r-bar}, and an uncontrollable distractor cart-pole as s^{a-bar r-bar}; comparable to Denoised MDP on RoboDesk and better on DMC distractor variants. — [ar5iv IFactor full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2306.06561)
- IFactor versus Denoised MDP: IFactor argues that uncontrollable but reward-relevant variables are also needed for the policy, and that Denoised MDP's assumed independent latent processes with instantaneous effects are unidentifiable without interventions. — [ar5iv IFactor full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2306.06561)
- AC-State (Lamb et al., 2022): a multi-step inverse model predicting actions from observations at two time points is claimed to recover the controllable (control-endogenous) latent with no dependence on exogenous noise, under the deterministic Ex-BMDP model. (Search snippet.) — [arXiv 2207.08229](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.08229)
- "Multistep Inverse Is Not All You Need" (Levine, Stone, Zhang; 2024): AC-State's required path length can be much larger than claimed and multistep inverse is insufficient under periodic dynamics regardless of k; ACDF adds a latent forward model and is claimed correct for a large class of Ex-BMDPs. An Ex-BMDP can have multiple valid endogenous/exogenous factorisations. (Search snippet.) — [arXiv 2403.11940](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.11940v2)
- Iso-Dream separates controllable from noncontrollable dynamics with inverse dynamics on isolated branches; RePo removes distractors by an information bottleneck plus predictability without modelling them. (Search snippets.) — [arXiv 2205.13817](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.13817); [arXiv 2309.00082](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.00082)

### Inferences
- Given a graph over named nodes including actions and reward, the IFactor partition is computed mechanically: a node is "controllable" if it has an incoming action edge and "reward-relevant" if it has a directed path to reward. A distractor (e.g. a second, uncontrolled pendulum) is a connected component with neither. Hidden physical parameters (mass, length) are nodes with no parents and no dynamics, which the four-way scheme would file under uncontrollable, reward-relevant.
- IFactor's own cart-pole labelling (cart position controllable, pole angle "not controllable") shows the direct-edge definition at work: the pole angle is influenced by the action only through other variables. A variable-level graph is strictly finer than the four-way partition and would need per-node blocks, where IFactor's theory only provides block-level guarantees.
- The AC-State critique about periodic dynamics is relevant to pendulum-like systems (Acrobot, cart-pole swing), which are periodic; inverse-model-only controllable-state discovery may be unreliable there. This is an inference from the stated failure condition, not a tested result.

### Gaps
- ACRO (offline RL variant of AC-State) was not researched.
- No source addresses hidden physical parameters (context variables) as latent blocks in these factored Dreamer models.
- Venue of AC-State and of the Levine et al. paper not verified.

## 7. Limits of forced factorisation; evidence on articulated systems

### Takeaway
Documented limits are: independent or block priors conflict with causally correlated factors (DEAR), non-uniqueness of role partitions without extra regularisers (Denoised MDP, Ex-BMDP), dependence on a frozen object encoder and loss of performance when slots replace patches (C-JEPA's OC-DINO-WM drop), and difficulty with multiple instances or non-object state. Evidence on articulated single-body systems is thin: one video cart-pole identifiability paper, IFactor's modified cart-pole, and DMC distractor benchmarks; I found no object-centric or variable-factored world model evaluated on Acrobot.

### Cited Findings
- Replacing patch latents with object slots without object-masked training cost planning performance on Push-T: DINO-WM 91.33%, OC-DINO-WM 60.67%, OC-JEPA 76.00%; C-JEPA recovers to 88.67%, still slightly below patch-based DINO-WM. C-JEPA's performance is capped by encoder quality. — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11389v2)
- C-JEPA reports that performance drops with excessive masking of objects. — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11389v2)
- Slots are permutation-equivariant, so C-JEPA needs an unmasked first-step "identity anchor" to keep slot identity. — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11389v2)
- OC-STORM's stated limitations reportedly include difficulty when multiple instances of an object matter. (Third-party summary; flagged by the search tool as needing confirmation.) — [arXiv 2501.16443](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.16443v1)
- Object-centric manipulation literature notes that slots lack alignment with robot or scene dynamics, and a 2026 paper argues standard visual encoders are blind to robot configuration and conditions slots on joint states via FiLM. (Search snippets; individual papers not opened.) — [arXiv 2601.21416](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.21416); [slot review summary](https://www.themoonlight.io/review/spotlighting-task-relevant-features-object-centric-representations-for-better-generalization-in-robotic-manipulation)
- DEAR Proposition 4: a factorised prior can make an entangled encoder the loss minimiser when true factors are causally correlated. — [ar5iv DEAR full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2010.02637)
- Denoised MDP: the factorisation is not unique without the minimality regulariser. Ex-BMDPs admit multiple valid factorisations. — [ar5iv Denoised MDP](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2206.15477); [arXiv 2403.11940](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.11940v2)
- CauCA: even with the correct graph, imperfect interventions leave ancestor mixing, and performance degrades slightly with more mixing layers in synthetic experiments. — [ar5iv CauCA full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.17225)
- Set-structured latent abstraction on DreamerV3 underperformed default DreamerV3 in a student project. (Weak source.) — [CS224R project PDF](https://cs224r.stanford.edu/projects/pdfs/Set_Dreamer_CS224R_Final_Project.pdf)
- Articulated-system evidence: (a) Hızlı et al. cart-pole from video, MCC 0.95, future-state MCC 0.93, drop to about 0.60-0.65 under unseen gravity before adaptation, no downstream control; (b) IFactor modified CartPole block recovery and DMC distractor variants; (c) Denoised MDP on DMC Cheetah/Walker/Reacher with noise. — [arXiv 2406.03337 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.03337v2); [ar5iv IFactor](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2306.06561); [ar5iv Denoised MDP](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2206.15477)
- A search specifically for slot/object-centric failures on articulated joint angles or DMC returned no direct result. — [search hit list example: slots4robots](https://sites.google.com/view/slots4robots)

### Inferences
- Object-centric structuring is a poor fit for cart-pole/Acrobot: the graph nodes are generalised coordinates and their rates of one articulated body, not separable image regions. All demonstrated slot world models (SOLD, C-JEPA, OC-STORM, STICA) are multi-object scenes. The alignment mechanism for articulated variables would have to be supervision-based, not segmentation-based.
- Every identifiability result leaves at least a permutation/element-wise ambiguity, and the block-level results (IFactor, CauCA fat-hand) leave within-block mixing. For a per-variable graph this means mixing among, for example, theta and theta-dot within a block is not ruled out by theory unless labels or per-variable interventions are supplied.
- Capacity loss from factorisation is plausible but not quantified in the sources for RSSM/JEPA on control tasks; the only quantitative datum is the Push-T slot-versus-patch gap.

### Gaps
- No results found for Acrobot in any structured-latent world model paper.
- No controlled study found of the performance cost of block-factorising DreamerV3 or a JEPA predictor on DMC.
- Slot-misbinding failure analyses in object-centric world models were not located in primary sources during this session.
- OC-STORM's limitation statement needs confirmation from the paper.
