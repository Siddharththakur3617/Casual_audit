# Causal dynamics / causal world models in MBRL: prior work closest to the pixels-only JEPA causal-graph study

Verification note: entries marked **[verified]** had their details checked against the primary page in this session. Entries marked **[from memory]** are well-known papers whose details come from the researcher's prior knowledge and were not re-fetched; the links are believed correct but the report writer should spot-check any specific number before quoting it.

## Q1. Causal dynamics learning in MBRL (CDL, GRADER, ASR, CAI, CoDA/MoCoDA, ELDEN, IFactor, OOCDM, CBM, CausalWorld): do these learn or evaluate graphs, and how close are they?

### Takeaway
Almost all of this literature **learns** a sparse or causal dynamics model, with the structure built into training through masks, CMI tests, or sparsity regularisers. Most methods work on **low-dimensional, factored state vectors**, and some only score the recovered graph against a hand-specified ground truth as a side metric. None of them takes an unconstrained, pixels-only world model and extracts its graph post hoc with statistical false-positive control and an untrained-model floor, which is what the study does. The closest in spirit are CDL/CBM, which score edges by CMI against a known graph, and the 2025 "Dynamic Sparsity" paper, which takes ground-truth graphs from simulator Jacobians and asks whether plain MLPs recover them.

### Cited Findings

**CDL: Causal Dynamics Learning for Task-Independent State Abstraction.** Z. Wang, X. Xiao, Z. Xu, Y. Zhu, P. Stone. ICML 2022 (PMLR 162:23151–23180). [ICML page](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/wang22ae.html) · [arXiv 2206.13452](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.13452) **[verified: venue, authors, abstract]**
- LEARNS a causal dynamics model that "removes unnecessary dependencies between state variables and the action", then derives a task-independent state abstraction from it. Evaluated on "two simulated environments and downstream tasks" — [ICML page](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/wang22ae.html)
- An edge is decided by a conditional-independence test. The test is implemented by a network that estimates **conditional mutual information** (CMI) while the dynamics model is learned — [CBM paper / survey summary via search](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.12497)
- [from memory] The environments are a "Chemical" block environment (from Ke et al. 2021) and a robosuite-based manipulation environment. Inputs are factored **state vectors**, not pixels. The ground-truth graph is known by construction in Chemical and hand-derived in manipulation. Graph accuracy is reported against it, and the CMI threshold is a fixed hyperparameter, not null-calibrated.
- Closeness: **partially**. It shares the per-edge scoring of state/action → next-state-variable edges against a known graph, and its CMI is conceptually similar to LOCO importance (compare prediction with vs without a parent). It differs because the graph is built into training, inputs are states, and there is no calibrated FWER control or untrained-model floor.

**CBM: Building Minimal and Reusable Causal State Abstractions for RL.** Z. Wang, C. Wang, X. Xiao, Y. Zhu, P. Stone. AAAI 2024. [arXiv 2401.12497](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.12497) **[verified via search snippet]**
- LEARNS shared task-agnostic causal dynamics plus a minimal task-specific abstraction. It estimates CMI with implicit models, which the authors report "improves predictive accuracy and causal identification" — [arXiv 2401.12497](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.12497v1)
- Closeness: **partially**, for the same reasons as CDL.

**GRADER: Generalizing Goal-Conditioned RL with Variational Causal Reasoning.** W. Ding, H. Lin, B. Li, D. Zhao. NeurIPS 2022. [arXiv 2207.09081](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.09081) **[from memory]**
- LEARNS a causal graph over state/goal variables jointly with the dynamics model, as a variational EM alternating between graph discovery and policy learning. Inputs are low-dimensional states. Environments have hand-specified ground-truth graphs (e.g. a chemistry-style environment and manipulation tasks), and graph recovery is shown against them.
- Closeness: **loosely to partially**. It has a known-graph answer key but learns the graph, uses state inputs, and has no post hoc extraction.

**ASR: Action-Sufficient State Representation Learning for Control with Structural Constraints.** B. Huang, C. Lu, L. Leqi, J. M. Hernández-Lobato, C. Glymour, B. Schölkopf, K. Zhang. ICML 2022. [arXiv 2110.05721](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.05721) **[from memory; the arXiv id should be double-checked]**
- LEARNS a structured latent world model from **high-dimensional observations, including images** (e.g. CarRacing, VizDoom). Learnable binary structural masks encode which latent dimensions affect which, and which affect reward. The goal is a minimal set of latent variables sufficient for control, not graph recovery against a simulator. There is no simulator ground-truth graph over physical variables.
- Closeness: **loosely**. It is pixel-based, but the structure is in latent space and is not compared against an expert physical graph.

**CAI / Causal Influence Detection for Improving Efficiency in Reinforcement Learning.** M. Seitzer, B. Schölkopf, G. Martius. NeurIPS 2021. [arXiv 2106.03443](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.03443) · [code](https://github.com/martius-lab/cid-in-rl) **[verified: authors, venue, CMI-based measure, robotic manipulation]**
- Introduces "a measure of situation-dependent causal influence" based on CMI between action and next state, given the current state. It is used to detect states where the agent's action has influence and to guide exploration and off-policy learning — [arXiv 2106.03443](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.03443)
- [from memory] It is estimated from a learned (state-based) transition model. Detection quality is evaluated against simulator-derived influence labels in Fetch manipulation tasks (e.g. contact between gripper and object), reported as precision-recall / AP-style metrics.
- Closeness: **partially**. It is one of the few works that evaluates a model-derived influence score against simulator ground truth with ROC/PR-type metrics. It concerns a single edge type (action → object), however, and is local, state-based, and not FWER-controlled.

**CoDA: Counterfactual Data Augmentation using Locally Factored Dynamics.** S. Pitis, E. Creager, A. Garg. NeurIPS 2020. [arXiv 2007.02863](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.02863) **[from memory]**
- Introduces **local causal models** (LCMs): the interaction graph can be sparse locally even when it is dense globally. LCMs are inferred from the attention weights of a transformer dynamics model, or from ground-truth masks, and are used to splice counterfactual transitions. Inputs are states (Spriteworld, Pong, Fetch). Mask quality is checked against simulator ground truth in some experiments.
- Closeness: **partially**. Here, too, a graph is read from a trained model, through attention, and compared with ground truth. The model is a structured attention model on states, however, and there are no calibrated tests.

**MoCoDA: Model-based Counterfactual Data Augmentation.** S. Pitis, E. Creager, A. Mandlekar, A. Garg. NeurIPS 2022. [arXiv 2210.11287](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.11287) **[from memory]**
- LEARNS a locally factored dynamics model, with factorisation given or inferred, to generate out-of-distribution counterfactual data. State inputs. Closeness: **loosely**.

**ELDEN: Exploration via Local Dependencies.** Z. Wang, J. Hu, P. Stone, R. Martín-Martín. NeurIPS 2023. [arXiv 2310.08702](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08702) **[existence and Jacobian use verified via citation in arXiv 2511.08086; other details from memory]**
- Infers local dependencies between state factors from the **Jacobian (partial derivatives) of a learned dynamics model**, with a regulariser that encourages sparse Jacobians, and uses the uncertainty of those dependencies as a curiosity signal. Cited by Pandaram et al. as one of the works that "examine Jacobians of learned dynamics models" — [arXiv 2511.08086](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08086v2)
- [from memory] Inputs are states. Some dependency-detection accuracy against simulator ground truth is reported (e.g. in kitchen/CookingMat-style environments).
- Closeness: **partially**. It extracts graphs from a trained model's input sensitivities, which is close to the study's post hoc edge scoring. The model is trained with a sparsity bias, however, and inputs are states.

**IFactor: Learning World Models with Identifiable Factorization.** Y.-R. Liu, B. Huang, Z. Zhu, H. Tian, M. Gong, Y. Yu, K. Zhang. NeurIPS 2023. [arXiv 2306.06561](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06561) **[from memory]**
- LEARNS a factorised latent world model from **pixels** (DMC, CarRacing, synthetic). The four latent blocks are split by whether they are action-relevant and/or reward-relevant, with block-wise identifiability theory. Evaluation is on latent-variable recovery (e.g. R² / MCC on synthetic data) and control performance, not on an edge graph over physical variables.
- Closeness: **loosely**. It is pixels-based, but its notion of structure is block-level latent factorisation, not variable-level edges against a simulator Jacobian.

**OOCDM: Learning Causal Dynamics Models in Object-Oriented Environments.** Z. Yu, J. Ruan, D. Xing. ICML 2024. [arXiv 2405.12615](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12615) **[verified: authors, venue, abstract]**
- LEARNS an object-oriented causal dynamics model in which objects of the same class share causal structure and parameters. It scales causal dynamics models beyond "small-scale environments". Evaluated on "causal discovery, prediction accuracy, generalization, and computational efficiency" — [arXiv 2405.12615](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12615)
- Closeness: **partially**. Graph recovery is scored, but the structure is learned and inputs are object-factored states.

**Task-Independent Causal State Abstraction.** Z. Wang et al., 2021 (NeurIPS 2021 Robot Learning workshop). It is a precursor of CDL and is cited in [arXiv 2511.08086](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08086v2). Closeness: **loosely**.

**Curious Causality-Seeking Agents Learn Meta Causal World.** Z. Zhao, …, J. Schmidhuber, M. Yang. NeurIPS 2025. [arXiv 2506.23068](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.23068) **[verified via search]**
- LEARNS a "Meta-Causal Graph" world model: several causal subgraphs, each triggered by a latent meta-state assigned by VQ. A curiosity-driven agent intervenes to verify edges. Tested on synthetic tasks and robot-arm manipulation — [NeurIPS 2025 poster](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/poster/115674)
- Pandaram et al. cite it as a work that examines Jacobians of learned dynamics models — [arXiv 2511.08086](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08086v2)
- Closeness: **loosely to partially**. Its state-dependent graphs and interventional verification are relevant to hidden-physics or regime confounding.

**Learning Local Causal World Models with State Space Models and Attention.** 2025. [arXiv 2505.02074](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.02074) **[verified only via search snippet; authors not checked]**
- LEARNS a causal graph over object/environment slots, with per-variable transition functions, in Pong-style environments. Reconstructed frames are shown next to the learned graph. Closeness: **loosely**.

**Causal Process Models: Reframing Dynamic Causal Graph Discovery as an RL Problem.** Orujlu et al., PMLR v323 (2026). [arXiv 2507.13920](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.13920) · [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v323/orujlu26a.html) **[verified only via search snippet]**
- RL agents build sparse, time-varying interaction graphs inside a predictive world model, **from visual observations**. Closeness: **loosely**. It is pixel-based, but the graph is learned and over objects.

**CausalWorld: A Robotic Manipulation Benchmark for Causal Structure and Transfer Learning.** O. Ahmed, F. Träuble, A. Goyal, A. Neitz, M. Wüthrich, Y. Bengio, B. Schölkopf, S. Bauer. ICLR 2021. [arXiv 2010.04296](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.04296) **[from memory]**
- A benchmark, not a method: TriFinger-robot block tasks in which causal variables (masses, sizes, friction, colours, goal poses) can be intervened on, for studying transfer and generalisation. It **does not** score learned models' causal graphs. Its "do-interventions on hidden physical parameters" mirrors the study's per-episode hidden physics. Closeness: **loosely**.

**Systematic Evaluation of Causal Discovery in Visual Model-Based RL.** N. R. Ke, A. Didolkar, S. Mittal, A. Goyal, G. Lajoie, S. Bauer, D. Rezende, Y. Bengio, M. Mozer, C. Pal. NeurIPS 2021 Datasets & Benchmarks. [arXiv 2107.00848](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.00848) **[from memory]**
- Introduces the "Physics" and "Chemistry" block environments, which are **rendered as images**. Their **ground-truth causal graphs are set by construction** (random DAGs over objects). The paper compares monolithic vs modular/graph-structured world models trained from pixels on prediction metrics (e.g. ranking/H@1, MRR, likelihood) over multiple steps. It also notes that the graph structure strongly affects difficulty. It mainly evaluates **predictive performance as a function of the ground-truth graph**, not post hoc extraction of the learned model's graph.
- Closeness: **partially, and among the closest on the pixel-input + ground-truth-graph axis**. It is the CDL "Chemical" environment's origin. It differs in having no edge-level extraction from an unconstrained model, no FWER control, and object-level graphs drawn at random instead of physics Jacobians.

**Dynamic Sparsity: Challenging Common Sparsity Assumptions for Learning World Models in Robotic RL Benchmarks.** M. Pandaram, J. Hollenstein, D. Drexel, S. Tosatto, A. Rodríguez-Sánchez, J. Piater. arXiv preprint, Nov 2025 (no venue listed). [arXiv 2511.08086](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.08086) **[verified]**
- Ground-truth causal structure comes from **simulator Jacobians**: MJX (differentiable MuJoCo) is auto-differentiated to get state and action Jacobians on about 11M steps of PPO-expert trajectories per environment. An element counts as zero if |J| < 1e-12, and "globally zero" means zero in all samples (a necessary condition, so an upper bound on true sparsity) — [HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08086v2)
- Environments: eight DMC tasks from MuJoCo Playground (BallInCup, **CartpoleBalance**, CheetahRun, ReacherHard, FingerSpin, FingerTurnEasy, WalkerRun, SwimmerSwimmer6). Acrobot and pendulum are not included — [HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08086v2)
- Findings: "global sparsity is rare". Sparsity is instead local, state-dependent, and clustered in time (e.g. during contacts) — [abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.08086)
- Learned-model test: a 2×512 ELU MLP trained on **ground-truth states** recovers some sparsity, but it falls short of ground truth by "multiple orders of magnitude", even with Jacobian-matching losses. MLP Jacobian entries count as zero if below 1e-6. Pixels are explicitly excluded because observations are not invertible functions of state, so their Jacobians generally don't exist — [HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08086v2)
- Closeness: **very close on the answer-key axis, partial overall**. Like the study, it builds the expert graph from exact simulator Jacobians, including cart-pole, and asks whether an unconstrained trained network reproduces the zeros. It differs in using states only, raw Jacobian thresholding (no LOCO, decoys, or null calibration), no untrained floor, no hidden-physics confounding, and only sparsity counts rather than F1/AUROC of edges. Its finding that unconstrained MLPs do not reproduce exact zeros is a useful counterpoint: the study's "absent = exactly zero" edges need statistical rather than exact-zero criteria.

### Inferences
- Across Q1, the dominant paradigm is "**build the causal structure into the model and check that it was recovered**" (CDL, CBM, GRADER, OOCDM, ELDEN, meta-causal graph). The study's paradigm is "**train an unconstrained general-purpose world model, then ask post hoc whether its read-out dynamics obey the simulator's graph**". This inversion is the study's main point of novelty relative to MBRL causal-dynamics work.
- CMI-based edge scores (CDL, CAI) are conceptually the closest relatives of LOCO importance: both compare predictive performance with vs without a candidate parent. These works threshold CMI heuristically, whereas the study uses null-calibrated max-T and decoys.
- Pixel-based structured models (ASR, IFactor, Ke et al., Causal Process Models) define structure over **latent blocks or objects**, not over named physical variables with a Jacobian-derived answer key. The study's choice to read out physical variables and score against simulator Jacobians is therefore distinctive.

### Gaps
- I could not extract CDL's exact graph-accuracy numbers and threshold values; the PDF text could not be parsed in this session.
- The exact ground-truth labels and metrics in Seitzer et al. (CAI), GRADER, ELDEN and OOCDM were not re-verified and come from memory.
- The ASR arXiv id (2110.05721) is from memory and should be checked.

## Q2. Theory: must robust agents / world models be causal? (Richens & Everitt and related)

### Takeaway
Richens & Everitt (ICLR 2024) prove that agents which stay low-regret under a wide range of distribution shifts must have learned an approximate causal model of the data-generating process. The result motivates the study's question but offers no empirical graph-extraction method. Recent surveys formalise identifiability of causal world models without benchmarking trained models.

### Cited Findings
- **Robust Agents Learn Causal World Models.** J. Richens, T. Everitt. ICLR 2024. [arXiv 2402.10877](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10877) **[from memory]**. Theoretical: any agent that achieves bounded regret under a sufficiently large set of distributional shifts (interventions) must have learned an approximate causal model, and the causal model can be recovered from the policy. There is no ground-truth graph experiment on trained neural world models, though a small synthetic illustration exists. Closeness: **loosely, as motivation**.
- **A Unifying Perspective on Causal World Models: From Observations to Representations to Structure.** arXiv 2608.13456 (2026). [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13456) **[verified abstract only]**. A survey/theory paper that clarifies "when the components of a WM can be recovered from data and up to which equivalence" and links causal world models to SCMs and causal discovery. Its abstract describes no empirical metric for the causal structure of trained world models — [arXiv 2608.13456](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13456). A search snippet notes that it distinguishes graphs over latent states from graphs over observed variables, and that Dreamer-style latents need not have a causal interpretation — [search result](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.13456)
- **Transformer Is Inherently a Causal Learner.** arXiv 2601.05647 (2026). [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.05647) **[verified via search snippet only]**. It claims that for decoder-only transformers trained autoregressively on time series, under standard assumptions, **gradient-based sensitivities to lagged inputs identify the true parents**, so post hoc attributions of an unconstrained trained model can reveal the causal graph. It also says attention weights are weaker than gradient attributions, and that attribution is biased toward recent lags even when earlier lags are true parents — [arXiv 2601.05647](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.05647)
  - Closeness: **partially to very close in logic**. This is the clearest published argument that post hoc input-importance on an *unconstrained* sequence model recovers the causal graph. It concerns observed time series, not pixels. The recency-bias finding is directly relevant to the study's u_{t−1}, u_{t−2} lag edges.
- **A survey on causal reinforcement learning.** Y. Zeng, R. Cai, F. Sun, L. Huang, Z. Hao. arXiv 2302.05209 (IEEE TNNLS). [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05209) **[from memory; the id should be double-checked]**. Organises causal RL into causal representation learning, causal-structure/dynamics learning, counterfactual data augmentation, etc. It lists CDL, CoDA, ASR and similar work, and contains no category for post hoc auditing of unconstrained world models.

### Inferences
- The theory says robust agents must be causal. The study tests empirically whether a *non-robust-by-design* predictive JEPA trained on observational data happens to be causal at the level of its read-out dynamics. Richens & Everitt would predict no guarantee without exposure to shifts. Per-episode hidden physics is a mild form of distribution shift, so it is a natural bridge.

### Gaps
- I found no empirical paper that tests the Richens–Everitt prediction on a trained neural world model with a simulator ground-truth graph.

## Q3. Relational inference and causal discovery from trajectories (NRI, ACD, neural Granger, physical-system benchmarks)

### Takeaway
NRI, ACD and neural Granger causality **learn** an interaction graph from trajectories, usually with a structured model (latent edge types or sparse input layers). They score it against ground-truth graphs from simulated physics, mostly springs, charged particles and Kuramoto, with AUROC or accuracy. Their metrics and synthetic physics answer keys parallel the study, but they are discovery methods, not audits of a pre-trained world model. Li et al. (2020) do this from video keypoints. CausalDynamics (NeurIPS 2025) is the current large benchmark.

### Cited Findings
- **NRI: Neural Relational Inference for Interacting Systems.** T. Kipf, E. Fetaya, K.-C. Wang, M. Welling, R. Zemel. ICML 2018. [arXiv 1802.04687](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.04687) **[from memory]**. A VAE whose latent variable is a discrete interaction graph; a GNN decoder predicts trajectories. Trained on **state trajectories** of springs, charged particles and Kuramoto oscillators (plus motion capture and NBA data). Edge-type accuracy is measured against simulator ground-truth graphs. Closeness: **partially**. It shares the ground-truth physics answer key and edge metrics, but the graph is learned explicitly, covers object–object interactions, and has no actions.
- **Amortized Causal Discovery (ACD).** S. Löwe, D. Madras, R. Zemel, M. Welling. CLeaR 2022. [arXiv 2006.10833](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.10833) **[from memory]**. It amortises Granger-causal graph inference across samples with different graphs but shared dynamics, as an NRI-style encoder. On springs, particles, Kuramoto and netsim it reports **AUROC** against ground-truth graphs, and it handles a hidden confounder variant. Closeness: **partially**. The amortisation across episodes with different hidden parameters parallels per-episode hidden physics, and AUROC is shared. It is still a structured discovery model on states.
- **Neural Granger Causality.** A. Tank, I. Covert, N. Foti, A. Shojaie, E. Fox. IEEE TPAMI 2021 (arXiv 2018). [arXiv 1802.05842](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.05842) **[from memory]**. cMLP/cLSTM models with group-sparse penalties on input weights. A series is a Granger cause if its input group is non-zero. AUROC against ground truth on Lorenz-96, VAR and DREAM. Closeness: **partially**. Its "input importance → edge" logic is close to LOCO, but the sparsity is built into training rather than tested post hoc.
- **Causal Discovery in Physical Systems from Videos.** Y. Li, A. Torralba, A. Anandkumar, D. Fox, A. Garg. NeurIPS 2020. [arXiv 2007.00631](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00631) **[from memory]**. Unsupervised keypoints are extracted from **video**, and an NRI-style inference module then infers the causal graph over keypoints, with ground truth from simulators (multi-body cloth/rope, mass-spring). It handles counterfactual prediction under hidden physical parameters. Closeness: **partially, and among the closest on pixels + physics ground truth**. The graph is learned explicitly over keypoints, not extracted from an unconstrained world model, and there is no action conditioning or FWER control.
- **Causal World Models by Unsupervised Deconfounding of Physical Dynamics.** M. Li, M. Yang, F. Liu, X. Chen, Z. Chen, J. Wang. arXiv 2012.14228 (2020). [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.14228) **[verified via search snippet]**. It learns object-oriented abstractions with GNN transitions, and do-interventions on abstract state variables predict counterfactual futures in physics settings with confounders — [search result](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.14228). Closeness: **loosely**. It is relevant to the study's hidden-physics confounding diagnostics.
- **CausalDynamics: A Large-Scale Benchmark for Structural Discovery of Dynamical Causal Models.** B. Herdeanu, J. Nathaniel, C. Roesch, J. Buch, G. Ramien, J. Haux, P. Gentine. NeurIPS 2025 Datasets & Benchmarks. [arXiv 2505.16620](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.16620) · [NeurIPS](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/39a3f7dc00e4723a5ca7808b04fc4d4f-Abstract-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.html) **[verified]**. Ground-truth graphs come from thousands of linearly and nonlinearly coupled ODEs/SDEs plus two idealised climate models, more than 5,300 graphs and 50M+ samples in total. State-of-the-art causal discovery algorithms are benchmarked on noisy, confounded and lagged dynamics — [arXiv v1/v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16620v2). Closeness: **loosely to partially**. It is a ground-truth-graph benchmark for dynamics, but for causal discovery algorithms on observed variables, not for world models. It has no control-specific cart-pole or Acrobot graphs.
- Explicit causal-discovery-on-cart-pole/pendulum benchmarks with a Jacobian-derived ground truth: none found other than Pandaram et al.'s CartpoleBalance analysis above — [arXiv 2511.08086](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08086v2)

### Inferences
- The study's metric choice (F1, AUROC, false positives against a physics ground-truth graph) is standard in this literature, which supports comparability. Its use of LOCO with decoys and max-T null calibration is more statistically conservative than the heuristic thresholds or AUROC-only reporting typical of NRI, ACD and neural Granger.
- A natural extra baseline that reviewers may ask for: run neural Granger or ACD directly on the true state trajectories (x, v, θ, ω, u) as an "oracle discovery" upper reference, alongside the untrained floor.

### Gaps
- I did not find "causal discovery from learned model Jacobians" papers in physics beyond ELDEN (MBRL) and the Pandaram et al. analysis.

## Q4. Does any work evaluate whether a trained general-purpose world model (Dreamer, JEPA, video model, transformer) encodes the ENVIRONMENT's causal graph with a simulator ground truth? Causal-metric and benchmark papers for world models.

### Takeaway
I found **no published work** that does all of the study's components together: an unconstrained, pixels-only world model, post hoc edge-level extraction of the environment's causal graph, an exact-simulator answer key, statistical false-positive control and an untrained-model floor. Existing 2025–2026 world-model "causal" benchmarks are **behavioural or interventional**: they test predicted outcomes under interventions or counterfactuals, not the edge structure of the model's dynamics. The closest edge-level analysis of trained unconstrained networks is state-based (Pandaram et al. 2025; the 2026 transformer-attribution paper).

### Cited Findings
- **What-If World: A Causal Benchmark for General World Models in Embodied Scenarios.** arXiv 2605.27589 (2026). [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27589) **[verified via search snippet only]**. A video world-model benchmark of contrastive interventions in driving and robot manipulation, with 319 paired triplets anchored on real nuScenes/DROID frames. All tested models fail on a large fraction of the interventions — [search result](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.27589). Closeness: **loosely**. It is interventional and behavioural, has no causal graph and no simulator Jacobian.
- **WMEval: Evaluating Interactive World Models Grounded on Physical Simulations.** arXiv 2610.33894 (2026). [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33894) **[verified via search snippet only]**. It runs identical action sequences in a Unity physics simulator and in the world model and compares states, finding gaps hidden by perceptual metrics. Closeness: **loosely**. It is simulator-grounded but evaluates prediction fidelity, not graph structure.
- **CausalVAE as a Plug-in for World Models: Towards Reliable Counterfactual Dynamics.** arXiv 2604.07712 (2026). [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.07712v1) **[verified via search snippet only]**. It evaluates world models under interventional protocols, with ground-truth counterfactual futures obtained by re-simulating from the intervened state. Closeness: **loosely**. It is interventional, but the structure is added to the model rather than audited.
- **ScratchWorld: Evaluating If World Models Compute Executable Consequences.** arXiv 2606.31689 (2026). [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.31689) **[verified via search snippet only]**. It argues that overlap-based future-state scoring is insufficient and checks causal-chain attribution and counterfactuals against runtime traces. Closeness: **loosely**. Its world models are LLM-style models over Scratch programs.
- **Virtual Cells as Causal World Models: A Perspective on Evaluation.** NeurIPS 2025 workshop/perspective. [NeurIPS](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/loc/san-diego/129556) **[verified via search snippet only]**. It proposes metrics for intervention validity, counterfactual consistency, trajectory faithfulness and "mechanistic alignment". Closeness: **loosely**, as a conceptual parallel to "mechanistic alignment" with an expert graph.
- **Causal-JEPA: Learning World Models through Object-Level Latent Masking.** H. Nam, Q. Le Lidec, L. Maes, Y. LeCun, R. Balestriero. ICML 2026 (arXiv 2602.11389). [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11389) **[verified]**. An object-centric JEPA with object-level latent masking. It reports about 20% absolute gains on counterfactual VQA and comparable planning with about 1% of the latent features. It does **not** explicitly learn or evaluate a causal graph — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11389). Closeness: **loosely**. It is the nearest JEPA work using "causal" framing, but it is not a graph audit.
- **Ke et al. 2021** (Q1) remains the closest pixel-based world-model study with ground-truth graphs. It evaluates prediction quality as a function of the graph, not the model's extracted graph — [arXiv 2107.00848](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.00848) **[from memory]**
- **Pandaram et al. 2025** (Q1) is the closest test of whether unconstrained trained dynamics networks reproduce simulator-Jacobian sparsity. It is state-based, and pixels are explicitly ruled out as non-invertible — [HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08086v2)
- **CAER: Causal Action Effect Reweighting for World Model Training.** arXiv 2608.30897 (2026), and **CausalDreamer** variants, arXiv 2610.12016 (factored latent between a frozen Dreamer-4-style tokenizer and the dynamics) and arXiv 2605.12312 (implicit causal graph for delay-aware RL). [CAER](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.30897) · [CausalDreamer](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.12016v1) · [delay-aware](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.12312) **[titles only, via search; contents not verified]**. All of these add causal structure during training rather than auditing a trained model post hoc, so they are **loosely related**.
- A broad search for "evaluating whether world models learn the causal graph of the environment" returned no paper that directly tests a trained world model's internal or read-out causal graph against the simulator's. The returned works test predictions under interventions instead — [search: WMEval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33894), [CausalVAE plug-in](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.07712v1)
- I found no paper named "CLAW" for causal evaluation of world models.

### Inferences
- Combining the four elements of the study — (a) a pixels-only, unconstrained world model, (b) post hoc edge-level graph extraction on a read-out of predicted state change, (c) a simulator-Jacobian answer key with strong/weak/absent tiers, and (d) statistical false-positive control (decoys plus null-calibrated max-T, FWER) with an untrained epoch-0 floor and a distractor providing truly-absent cross edges — appears to be **novel**, as far as these searches reached.
- Individually: (a) appears in ASR, IFactor, Ke et al. and Li et al. 2020, but with learned or structured graphs. (b) appears in CoDA (attention), ELDEN (Jacobian) and the 2026 transformer-attribution paper (gradients), but on states. (c) appears in Pandaram et al. 2025, but on states with MLP Jacobian thresholding. (d) has no equivalent: no MBRL causal-dynamics paper I found reports an untrained-model floor or FWER-calibrated edge pruning.
- The distractor-object control resembles the irrelevant or "uncontrollable" distractor variables CDL and IFactor use to show that structured models ignore nuisances. In the study, it is used instead as a source of known absent edges for false-positive accounting.

### Gaps
- Several 2026 arXiv items (What-If World, WMEval, ScratchWorld, CausalVAE plug-in, CAER, CausalDreamer) were seen only as search snippets. Their authors, venues and exact methods were not verified.
- OpenReview submissions under review (ICLR 2027 cycle) were not searched systematically. A post hoc causal-graph audit of Dreamer/JEPA may exist there unpublished.
